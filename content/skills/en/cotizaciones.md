## When to use

- You need to send a quote or estimate to a prospect or client for a job, project, or service — not a generic sales pitch.
- You can share the scope (what's included), your price or rate, and roughly how you get paid (deposit, milestones, net terms).
- You want the guardrails built in by default: a validity window, what's NOT included, and payment terms — not just nice-sounding prose.

Do not use it to invent a price, discount, or timeline you haven't decided. If you haven't set a price, the skill asks instead of guessing one.

## What it does

Builds a quote end to end:

- **Turns your scope into line items**: what you described as deliverables, not a vague paragraph a client has to interpret.
- **Sets a validity window** (e.g., "valid 15 days") so the price doesn't sit open forever and get used against you later.
- **Lists exclusions explicitly**: what's out of scope, so if the client asks for more later, there's a paper trail that it's a change order, not scope creep.
- **Adds payment terms**: deposit percentage, milestones or net-30, and what happens if payment is late — only from what you tell it, never invented defaults like "50% deposit" if you didn't say that.
- **Writes the sending message**: a short email/WhatsApp note to go with the quote, with a clear next step and a date to follow up.
- **Flags anything missing**: no price, no scope, no payment terms — it asks instead of filling the gap with a guess.
- **Closes with a verdict (always)**: a 0–100 score for how protected this quote is — expiry date, exclusions, and payment terms present — given what you shared; a traffic light (🟢 80+ ready to send; 🟡 50–79 send it, but close the flagged gaps first; 🔴 <50 missing pieces that expose you, fill them before sending); the single highest-impact gap to close; and the honesty caveat that the score is about how well-protected the document is, not a promise the client accepts the price or pays on time.

## How to use

1. Describe the job: scope/deliverables, your price or rate, and how you want to get paid.
2. Add any hard limits: what's explicitly excluded, your minimum deposit, how long the price holds.
3. Ask: *"Write me a quote for [job] at [price], valid 15 days, 50% deposit up front."*
4. Review the exclusions and payment terms before sending — they're your protection if the job changes.

## Best for

Freelancers, agencies, contractors, and service businesses that send quotes or estimates regularly and want every one to carry the same protections, without rewriting the boilerplate — or forgetting a clause — each time.
