## When to use

- You said "I want to hire someone" or "I need my first employee and don't know where to start".
- You've spent months doing everything yourself and the only way out you see is adding a person, but you can't define the role.
- You already know the role and want the job post, interview questions and reference checks, not a blank page.
- Résumés started arriving and you don't know how to screen them without interviewing everybody.

Don't use it to decide whether you can afford to hire (that depends on your numbers), or as labor advice: contract, registration and social-security obligations depend on your country and get checked with a professional or the local agency before the offer.

## What it does

- **Defines the role by tasks, not title**: starts from what you do today that isn't scaling and which weekly results define that the role works — "run the counter and the lunchtime orders without a backlog", not "seeking a proactive person".
- **Writes the job post ready to publish**: what the person will do, hours, schedule, location, what you offer and how to handle pay — posted, a range, or "depending on experience", with the tradeoff explained. No filler phrases that scare good people off.
- **Builds the interview questions**: 5 to 8 questions that test the role's actual tasks, each with what a good answer looks like and what is a red flag — plus a short practical test (15 to 30 minutes) where the role allows one.
- **Gives the reference script**: three concrete questions for calling previous employers, what to listen for and what disqualifies someone.
- **Sorts the résumé screening**: a rule for discarding on what matters (experience in those tasks, stated availability, concrete signals) and a block-scheduling plan so interviews stop eating your week.
- **Marks what depends on your country**: contract, tax registration, social security and total cost of employment are listed as "check this before the offer", without inventing country-specific requirements or legal amounts.

It doesn't tell you whether to hire yet, doesn't promise the hire will work out, and gives no score: this is a definition-and-writing process, not a closed business decision.

## How to use

1. Tell it what you do today, how many hours each task eats, and what you want to free up first.
2. Define the role together: tasks, hours, schedule, budget and how "this works" gets measured at 30 days.
3. Publish the job post that comes out of it and screen résumés against the real-tasks rule.
4. Interview with the questions and the practical test, writing down the signals — don't improvise interview by interview.
5. Call references with the script before making the offer.
6. Before offering the job, check your country's obligations (contract, registration, total cost) with a professional or the local agency — the skill tells you what to ask, it doesn't settle it for you.

**Usage tip:** hire for the task that jams you up the most, not the one you dislike the least. The skill will ask anyway: "what happens today if this task doesn't get done?"

## Best for

Business owners and shops making their first or second hire who have never run a selection process: defining the role, writing the post, interviewing with criteria and checking references without leaning on gut feeling. Not for corporate HR, and no substitute for labor advice in your country — the skill builds the process and the texts; the contract and registration get settled by a professional.
