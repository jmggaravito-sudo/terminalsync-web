## When to use

- You're deciding between two or three options — software, equipment, a service provider — and every comparison you find is a marketing table.
- A salesperson pushed a tool and you want a second read before you sign anything.
- A subscription you already use got more expensive and you're wondering whether to stay or switch.

Not for: picking between things you can't describe, or promising that a tool will grow your sales. It sharpens the decision; it doesn't predict outcomes.

## What it does

- **Starts from your job, not the catalog**: what you need the tool to do this month, what's a must-have and what's nice-to-have.
- **Compares against your must-haves**: if an option doesn't cover one, it gets disqualified — not counted as "same as the rest" by feature count.
- **Calculates the true cost of each**: the plan you'd realistically need (not the advertised base), per-seat and per-usage extras, setup and migration time, and annual-only discounts that lock you in.
- **Prices the exit before you enter**: how hard it is to leave each option — data export, what breaks, how much retraining your team needs — so you know what you're committing to.
- **Plans the trial**: how to test the front-runner for a week with real data instead of trusting the demo.
- **Closes with a verdict (always)**: a 0–100 score for how ready this decision is to make with the information you gave; a traffic light (🟢 80+ decide with what you have; 🟡 50–79 decide, but close the listed gaps first — confirm usage pricing, run the trial; 🔴 <50 key information is missing or there's a blocker, don't decide yet); the single highest-impact next action; and the honesty note: the score reflects what you told the model, not a promise of results — real numbers (invoices, usage, quotes) win.

## How to use

1. Say what you need it to do and your must-haves ("has to work offline", "has to talk to my invoicing").
2. Name the options you're between — two or three, with their prices or a link to their pricing page.
3. Add your constraints: budget ceiling, team size, what you already use today.
4. Ask: *"Which one do I pick?"* — you get the comparison plus the verdict.
5. If the verdict is 🟡 or 🔴, close the gaps it lists and ask again with that information.

## Best for

Owners without a procurement team who choose software, equipment, or providers themselves, and who pay the price of a wrong choice with their own money and time. Also for renewal calls: whether to keep paying more for what you have or pay the switching cost once.
