## When to use

- When you need to fill a role — server, cashier, admin assistant, general helper — and don't know how to write the post or what to ask in the interview.
- When you already have applicants and want to compare them with the same rule for everyone, not by gut feeling or charisma.
- When you want to stick to what predicts the job and avoid questions you shouldn't be asking (age, family status, health, nationality).
- Do not use it to make the decision by itself: the final hire is yours. And for the legal side of employment (contracts, contributions, benefits) check your country's rules or talk to an accountant or employment lawyer — this is not legal advice.

## What it does

- **Writes the job post**: from what you tell it about the role (real tasks of a typical day, schedule, pay or range, contract type), it produces a clear post: what the person does, what's required, what you offer, and how to apply.
- **Builds questions per role**: behavioral questions tied to the actual tasks ("tell me about a time you had to handle a full dining room alone"), plus a short practical exercise if the role allows for one. Not the generic "strengths and weaknesses" list.
- **Compares with the same rule**: if you share your notes on several applicants, it builds a table with the same criteria for everyone, marks what each one covered, and flags what remains unverified.
- **Asks for what's missing**: if you haven't defined pay, schedule, or exact tasks, it points that out before you publish — a vague post brings in applicants you weren't looking for.
- **Does not filter by who the person is**: it rejects criteria based on age, gender, family status, health, or nationality; it compares experience and performance on the role's tasks.

## How to use

1. Describe the role: tasks from a typical day, schedule, pay or range, and what is breaking today with how you currently work.
2. Publish the post it builds and use its questions in the interviews; take brief notes on each applicant.
3. Paste those notes so it builds the comparison with the same criteria, check what remains unverified, and make the call yourself.

## Best for

Business owners who hire without an HR department: shops, restaurants, workshops, service businesses. Especially useful for a first hire, or when hiring happens often enough that a repeatable process beats improvising every time.
