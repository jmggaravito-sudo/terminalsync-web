## When to use

- Every time you find yourself explaining your business again: what you sell, to whom, at what prices, and what you decided last week.
- When you use the AI for different tasks on different days — a quote today, a post tomorrow, a customer message the day after — and want them written with your business's facts, not generic filler.
- When something changes — you raised a price, signed a new customer, switched suppliers — and you want it recorded in one place.
- Do not use it to store passwords, API keys, card numbers, or sensitive customer personal data: that belongs in your password manager or your accounting system, not in a brief that gets copied between conversations.

## What it does

- **Builds the brief in one conversation**: asks for what's missing (what you sell, average ticket, typical customers, suppliers, hours, business rules) and produces a one-page brief with only what you told it.
- **Keeps it current**: when you tell it about a change ("I raised the delivery fee", "that supplier no longer works with us"), it updates the brief, shows you exactly what changed, and stamps the date.
- **Separates confirmed from pending**: what you declared is recorded as a fact; what you don't know yet (exact margin, customer count) is flagged as "pending confirmation" instead of being filled in.
- **Delivers the brief ready to paste**: after every build or update, it shows the full brief so you can copy it into the place you already work — a fixed document, your meeting note, your business group chat.
- **Does not invent**: if a fact is missing, it asks or leaves it blank as pending; it never fills gaps with assumptions, other businesses' averages, or generic examples presented as yours.
- **Does not store secrets**: if asked to keep passwords, keys, or card numbers, it refuses and suggests where that belongs instead.

## How to use

1. Say *"Build my business memory"* and answer its questions. If you already have the info written somewhere, paste it and it will organize it.
2. Keep the brief in one fixed place and, each time it updates it, replace the old version with the new one.
3. Day to day: paste the brief when starting an important task (a quote, a campaign, a delicate message), or ask it to *"update the memory"* whenever something changes, with the old brief at hand.

## Best for

Business owners who use the AI piecemeal — quotes one day, content the next, customer replies the day after — and lose context between conversations. Also useful when you delegate: the brief is the summary another person needs to answer customers the way you would.
