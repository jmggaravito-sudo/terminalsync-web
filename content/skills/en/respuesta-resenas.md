## When to use

- A customer just left a review — great, bad, or unfair — and you want a reply that protects your reputation without escalating.
- You have a batch of unanswered reviews and want each one answered differently, not the same "Thanks for your feedback!" paste.
- A review says something factually wrong and you don't know how much to correct in public.

Not for: writing fake positive reviews, mass-posting identical replies, or promising that a platform will remove a review. It drafts replies; it does not moderate, delete, or negotiate with platforms.

## What it does

- **Sorts the review first**: positive, justified complaint, misunderstanding, or likely fake — and asks when it can't tell from the text alone.
- **Positive → specific thanks**: it answers using the detail the customer actually mentioned (the dish, the delivery, the technician), in two or three sentences, instead of a generic thank-you.
- **Justified complaint → apology + fix**: acknowledges the specific thing that went wrong, says what you're changing, and moves the resolution (order number, refund, replacement) to a private channel with a real contact path.
- **Unfair or fake → firm, calm, short**: one factual correction without arguing, insults, or a thread war — written for the future customers who will read it, not to win against the reviewer.
- **Keeps public replies safe**: no other customers' names, no internal data, no legal admissions ("it was our fault, we're liable"), no refund offers posted where they can be screenshotted as binding. Those belong in a private message.
- **Adapts length and tone per platform**: Google, Facebook, MercadoLibre, TripAdvisor, or your booking site — short where readers skim.
- **Matches the reviewer's language** by default, and can deliver EN/ES pairs when you serve both.

## How to use

1. Paste the review, plus one or two facts from your side: what actually happened and what you can offer.
2. Say the platform and the tone you want (formal, close, playful).
3. Ask: *"Draft the reply to this review."* For a batch: *"Answer these five reviews, one reply each."*
4. Read the draft as a future customer would, correct anything that isn't exactly true, and post it from your own account. If it suggests taking the conversation private, actually send that message.

## Best for

Local businesses, restaurants, clinics, hotels, and online stores whose review page is their storefront and who get reviews weekly without a community manager. Also useful for owners who freeze on angry reviews and either go silent or over-answer.
