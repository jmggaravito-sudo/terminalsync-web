## When to use

- You said "will my money make it through the month?", "cash is tight" or "help me project my cash flow".
- A big payment is coming up (payroll, tax, rent, a supplier) and you want to know whether the cash holds before the date arrives.
- You are about to make a purchase or take on a new expense and want to see the impact on the coming weeks before deciding.
- You have money "on the way" (quotes sent, invoices pending) and don't know how much of it you can count today.

Don't use it as financial, credit or investment advice, or to decide to stop paying taxes, payroll or a loan — it organizes your numbers and shows where the tension is; the decision is yours, with your accountant.

## What it does

- **Builds the week-by-week table**: starts from your actual cash today and projects 4 to 8 weeks with what comes in and what goes out in each one, with the end-of-week balance computed step by step.
- **Separates the certain from the expected**: confirmed collections (issued invoices with dates, agreed recurring payments) in one column, and what will *probably* come in (quotes sent, likely sales) in a clearly marked separate one. It never adds the expected column as if it were collected.
- **Flags the tightest week**: the projected cash minimum and the points where you would go negative or below the buffer you define — the week that will squeeze you, with a date on it.
- **Measures the impact of what you want to do**: if you are about to buy something or take on an expense, it puts it in the table and shows how the minimum changes, instead of opining in the air.
- **Asks for what's missing instead of inventing it**: if you didn't give a number (how much you have today, when you get paid, payroll cost), it asks — or builds the scenario marking what it assumed.
- **Closes with a Veredicto**: a 0–100 score of how healthy your cash is based ONLY on the data you gave, with an explicit semaphore — 🟢 80+ cash covers the coming weeks with buffer; 🟡 50–79 some weeks are tight: act, but close these gaps first; 🔴 below 50 there's missing information or weeks in the red, you can't decide on this yet. It closes with **the single highest-impact action** (one only: for example "collect today the invoice due Thursday") and the honesty caveat: the score reflects what the skill could see, not a promise of what will happen — actual cash is what counts.

It never turns a possible sale into a confirmed collection, never omits a known expense to make the number look nice, and never tells you "you'll be fine" without showing the table behind it.

## How to use

1. Give the starting point: how much money you have available today (cash, bank, immediately collectable).
2. List what comes in with dates: issued invoices pending collection, recurring payments, and — separately — what will *probably* come in.
3. List what goes out with dates: payroll or your own draw, rent, suppliers, taxes, loan installments.
4. Say what buffer you want to keep (for example "never below one month of expenses") and, if there is a purchase or expense under study, how much and when.
5. Read the table and the Veredicto: the tightest week is the one to handle, not the month's average.
6. Act on the single highest-impact action and project again when something changes: one collection landing, a new expense or a moved date changes the verdict.

**If you're missing a number**, don't guess it: ask for the scenario with the assumption marked and replace it when you have the real figure. A projection with marked assumptions is useful; one with invented numbers is not.

## Best for

Business owners, freelancers and shops that live on the next few weeks' cash and want to see the tension before feeling it: the tight month, the purchase to postpone, the invoice to collect today. Not for someone looking for a guaranteed figure or permission to skip obligations — the skill shows the numbers you gave, separates the certain from the expected, and leaves the decision (and the margin of error) in your hands.
